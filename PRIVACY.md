## 1. What this service is

Legend Trade is a market-analysis terminal. It charts markets, computes
technical analysis, backtests strategies, prices options, and simulates trades.
It is a measurement tool, not financial advice — see the Terms of Service.

## 2. What we collect

Everything in this section is enumerated from the database schema
(`database/models.py`). There is no other collection path.

### Account data — because you gave it to us

| Data | Why | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Email address | Identifies your account; used for password reset and email verification | Required |
| Display name | Shown in the interface | Optional, may be blank |
| Password | Authentication | Stored **only** as a scrypt hash. We cannot recover or read your password. |
| Two-factor secret | Verifying your 2FA codes | Stored in symmetric form because verifying a future TOTP code mathematically requires reproducing it. Only present if you enable 2FA. |
| Two-factor recovery codes | Signing in when your authenticator is unavailable | Stored hashed, like a password |
| Webhook token | A secret URL segment for your TradingView alerts | Rotatable without changing your password |

### Security and account-integrity data — generated by using the service

Failed login count, account lock expiry, last login time, a "tokens valid from"
timestamp used to make sign-out actually revoke access, password-reset request
time, and email-verification time.

### Your trading activity — created by you using the service

Watchlists, price alerts, saved strategies and their audit verdicts, backtest
results, stored analyses (including the symbol, timeframe, price at the time,
and the full analysis payload), simulated paper positions, orders, feedback
ratings, and TradingView webhook payloads you send us.

Stored analyses are later scored against what price actually did. That scoring
is what makes the platform's probability estimates measurable rather than
decorative.

### Technical data

Your IP address appears in server logs and is used as a rate-limiting key to
stop brute-force attacks and abuse. Rate-limit buckets are transient. Log
retention is set by the operator — see [Retention](#6-how-long-we-keep-it).

### What we do **not** collect

- **No analytics, tracking pixels, advertising identifiers or third-party
  scripts of any kind.** The frontend loads no external JavaScript. This is
  verifiable: search the repository for `gtag`, `segment`, `mixpanel`,
  `posthog`, `sentry`, `fbq` — there are no matches.
- **No cookies.** Your session lives in your browser's `localStorage` under the
  keys `legend.access_token`, `legend.refresh_token` and `legend.user`. Signing
  out deletes them.
- **No payment or card details.** Payment processing is not implemented. If it
  is added later, this policy must be revised before it ships.
- **No broker credentials from you.** Broker access is configured
  instance-wide by the operator and is restricted to administrators. Ordinary
  accounts get simulated trading only and never supply broker keys.

## 3. Who we send data to

### Market-data providers

To draw a chart we ask a market-data vendor for that symbol's prices. **The
vendor learns which symbols this instance is looking at.** It does not receive
your email, your name, your account ID or any other identifier — the request
contains a symbol and a timeframe and nothing else. But on a small instance,
symbol traffic is behavioural information, and you should assume the vendor can
see it.

Depending on configuration, one or more of: Binance, Coinbase, Yahoo Finance,
Twelve Data, Polygon, Finnhub, Alpha Vantage, and FRED (US Federal Reserve
economic data).

### Language-model providers — optional, off by default

If the operator configures `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` or `GEMINI_API_KEY`, a completed
analysis can be turned into prose by Anthropic or Google. **The market analysis
is sent; your identity is not.** With neither key set, no request is ever made
and a deterministic template is used instead.

This is the *only* place this software calls a language model. No signal,
probability or risk decision consults a model — those are arithmetic on price
data.

### Email delivery

If SMTP is configured, your email address is transmitted to the operator's mail
provider in order to deliver password-reset and verification emails. Without
SMTP configured, reset links are written to the server log instead, which means
**the operator can see them**.

### Brokers

Alpaca, Tradier or Interactive Brokers, only for administrator accounts that
have deliberately enabled broker-connected trading. Order details are sent
under the operator's credentials.

### Nobody else

We do not sell your data. We do not share it with advertisers, data brokers or
partners. There is no code path that does so.

## 6. How long we keep it

Account and trading data are kept until the account is deleted. Deletion is
currently a manual operator action.

Server-log retention, including IP addresses, is whatever the operator's
infrastructure is configured for. **{{OPERATOR_LEGAL_NAME}} must state a real
number here** — "as long as necessary" is not a retention policy.

## 7. Security

- Passwords are hashed with scrypt. Recovery codes are hashed.
- Sessions use short-lived access tokens (30 minutes) with refresh tokens.
  Signing out and changing your password both revoke every existing token.
- Optional TOTP two-factor authentication with one-time recovery codes.
- Rate limiting on authentication endpoints, with account lockout after
  repeated failures.
- Authentication is enforced automatically whenever the server is reachable off
  its own machine.

**What is not claimed:** data is not encrypted at rest beyond whatever the
operator's disk encryption provides, and the two-factor secret must be stored
symmetrically for TOTP to work at all. If the database file is stolen,
passwords and recovery codes remain hashed, but two-factor secrets do not.

## What still needs a lawyer

This draft is accurate about the software. It is not a substitute for advice.
Before publishing, get a qualified professional to check:

1. **Which regimes apply**, given where you and your users are.
2. **Whether the operator is a controller, a processor, or both**, and whether
   a Data Processing Agreement is needed with any provider in section 3.
3. **International transfer mechanisms** — SCCs, adequacy, or otherwise.
4. **Retention periods** — sections 6 and 9 have placeholders that must become
   real numbers.
5. **Whether manual fulfilment of access/erasure requests is defensible** at
   your expected scale, or whether the endpoints must exist first.
6. **Whether giving the public trade setups and probabilities triggers
   financial-services regulation** in your jurisdiction. This is the single
   highest-risk open question and it is not a privacy question — it is a
   licensing one. Do not treat silence as permission.
